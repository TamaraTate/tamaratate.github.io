Technology and literacy

How students become better writers

I study how students develop as writers, what teachers can do to support them, and how technology shapes both. My current work asks where AI can help students plan, rethink, and revise their writing, and what they need to practice on their own.

Current research projects

WRITE AI Center

As Associate Director and Co-Principal Investigator, I help lead a national effort launched in September 2026 to study how generative AI can support writing instruction and AI literacy. Our team is developing a community-college writing intervention and plans to evaluate it in first-year composition courses.

PapyrusAI

I co-developed and lead research on UC Irvine’s guided AI writing platform. Instructors can structure interactions around activities such as planning an argument, reviewing feedback, and deciding what to revise.

NSF engineering writing study

As Principal Investigator, I worked with instructors to study how students use generative AI in an upper-division engineering writing course and how course design can help students practice the writing skills they need.

The WRITE AI Center is supported by the Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education, under Grant R305C260035.

About Tamara

I’m interested in what students are actually doing when they write with AI. Are they figuring out what they think? Trying a new approach? Getting past a sticking point? Those differences matter when we decide how to teach writing.

I’m an Associate Project Scientist and Associate Director of the Digital Learning Lab at UC Irvine, and Co-Principal Investigator and Associate Director of the IES-funded WRITE AI Center. I co-edit the Cambridge Elements series in Generative AI in Education. I hold a PhD and BA from UC Irvine and a JD from UC Berkeley.

I’m also the Principal Investigator on NSF IUSE: EDU Award 2315294.
